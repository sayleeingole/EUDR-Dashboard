User story — one assessment, start to sign

Guatemala, Start to Sign

A walk through every click of taking a new country — Guatemala, as an example — from an empty scope to a signed Article 10 risk assessment, an Article 9 legality baseline, and an exported checklist. Nothing here is hypothetical — every screen, button and label below is what the dashboard actually shows today.

Where the click-path actually goes — click a stop to jump there
→ → ⇄ ⇄ ⇄ →

The two dashed stops are the same Evidence tab, visited twice — every finding a research run makes, for either article, lands there first. You approve it there, then go back to Legal or Risk to see it turn into a requirement or a rating. That round trip is the back-and-forth you flagged — the map above is what it looks like laid flat.

Jump to
1
Login

Sign in

Every decision you make from here on is stamped with this name.

You see "EUDR country risk assessment · KLK Emmerich GmbH" and one field, Your name.
You click Type your name → Sign in.
Produces A signed-in session. This name is what shows up next to every approval, rejection, sign-off and export from here on — it's how the audit trail knows who did what.
2
Settings

Open the assessment cycle

Skip this if a cycle is already open — the top bar will say so.

You see A card titled Assessment cycles, listing any existing cycles and their status.
You click Enter a year → Open cycle.
Produces An open cycle. Nothing can be drafted or signed against a country until one exists — every other screen checks for this first.
3
Settings

Add Guatemala

Still on Settings, one card down.

You see A card titled Countries & regions, with fields for name and ISO3 code.
You click Type Guatemala, ISO3 GTM → Add country. (If you assess by plantation region rather than the whole country, the form below it adds a region under Guatemala the same way.)
Produces Guatemala now appears in the country switcher at the top of every page — country-level by default, empty of any evidence or ratings.
4
Any page

Switch scope to Guatemala

This dropdown sits in the header of every screen — it's what "scope" means throughout the app.

You click Top-right of the header, the country dropdown → select Guatemala. Leave the region dropdown on Country level.
Produces Every page you visit from now on — Overview, Art. 9, Evidence, Art. 10, Checklist — is scoped to Guatemala + this cycle until you change the dropdown again.
5
Overview

Land on the empty Overview

This is the default landing page — where "Overview" in the sidebar always takes you.

You see
  • Art. 9 requirements refined: 0 / 0 · Art. 10 sections signed: 0 / 10 · Complete: 0%
  • A Blockers to completion card — right now it just says there's no evidence yet
  • Ten section rows under Art. 10 sections, each pilled "Not signed"
This page is worth remembering as your home base: once real work is underway, its "What needs me now" card links straight to whatever's next — you don't have to remember which of the five tabs to open.
6
Art. 9 · Legality

Run legal research

This is where Article 9 actually starts — with a research run, not a blank form.

You click Open Art. 9 · Legality in the sidebar → expand Run research → tick the legal areas to cover (A1–A7 — leave all unchecked to run every area) → Run research.
You wait The run happens in the background — a banner at the top of the page tracks progress. It's calling Claude to research each legal area against Guatemalan law, so a full run across all seven areas takes a few minutes.
Produces Nothing on this page yet — every finding lands as a pending record in the Evidence tab, not here. That's the handoff the route map above shows.
7
Evidence

Review the legal findings

One record at a time, in a two-pane reading view.

You see A left-hand list grouped by legal area (A1, A2, A3…), each group showing its count. A summary line up top: "N pending across M areas." Click any row and the right-hand pane fills with that record — the claim, its source, confidence, and a link to read the source itself.
You click Per record: Approve, or one of the quick reject-reason buttons. Press J / K to move to the next / previous record without touching the mouse. If a source isn't yet on your trusted list, the pane asks you to name a reason and choose whether you're trusting the whole website or just this one article.
Produces Every record you approve becomes a candidate requirement — it moves out of Evidence and reappears back on Art. 9 · Legality, in that area's group, waiting to be refined.
8
Art. 9 · Legality

Refine each candidate requirement

Back on Art. 9 — the approvals from step 7 are now sitting here as rows to decide.

You see The same left-list-plus-reading-pane layout as Evidence. Header counters — Total, To refine, Relevant — track your progress across all seven areas.
You click Click a row → in the pane, fill in what the law text doesn't already answer: Applies to (plantation / smallholder / both), Supply chain node (chip checkboxes — source, mill, refinery, business partner), Documentation needed, Issuing authority, Mandatory? (yes / no / conditional), and — only if conditional — Condition. Then Accept or Reject. Accepting jumps you straight to the next candidate; J / K also work here.
Produces Once every candidate in an area is decided, To refine for Guatemala hits 0 and a Legality baseline preview card appears at the bottom of the page — the actual list of documents a Guatemalan supplier must be able to produce, grouped by which law demands each one.
9
Art. 10 · Risk

Run risk research

Same move as step 6, one tab over.

You click Open Art. 10 · Risk Assessment → expand Run research → tick which of the ten sections to run (S1–S10 — deforestation, governance, labour rights, and so on; leave blank for all) → Run research.
A few sections — forest cover, governance indicators, labour statistics — pull hard numbers directly from public APIs (FAOSTAT, Global Forest Watch, World Bank, ILO) before Claude researches the rest. You'll see a small 🔗 badge on those sections in the scope list.
Produces Same handoff as before — findings go to Evidence, not to this page.
10
Evidence

Review the risk findings

Same screen as step 7 — now grouped by S1 through S10 instead of A1 through A7.

You click Work down the list the same way: open a record, Approve / reject-with-reason, J / K to move on. For records you're confident about at a glance, tick their checkboxes in the list and Approve selected to clear several at once.
Produces Each approval adds one item to that section's approved-evidence count back on Art. 10 — visible there as "N approved · M pending" and a small evidence-mix bar (how much of it is official vs. civil-society vs. media sourced).
11
Art. 10 · Risk

Assess and sign a section — e.g. "Presence of forest"

This is the core act of Article 10: turning approved evidence into a rating you stand behind.

You see Ten section cards, each with its title, article reference, and evidence count. Expand Assess & sign on the one you're working — say, Presence of forest.
You click
  • Tick which listed rule conditions the evidence actually supports — each shows its plain-language wording and what rating it implies.
  • The page instantly shows a Suggested rating based on what you ticked, with the evidence count backing it.
  • Choose your Confirmed rating — it starts on the suggestion, but you can pick differently; doing so requires an override reason.
  • Write the narrative conclusion, or click Generate narrative draft to have it drafted from the approved evidence (about a minute), then edit it.
  • Save draft to keep working, or Sign to finalize.
Produces A signed assessment: version number, your name, the timestamp, and the narrative — permanently in the record for this cycle. The card's pill turns from "Not signed" to a colored rating pill, and the Overview progress bar moves.
12
Art. 10 · Risk

Repeat for the other nine sections

Same card, same five sub-steps, nine more times.

You click Scroll to the next card and repeat step 11 — Legal & regulatory framework, Governance, Land tenure, Forest cover, and so on through S10. If a section still has pending evidence, its card says so with a link straight back to Evidence, filtered to that section.
Produces A fully signed Article 10 risk assessment for Guatemala — ten ratings, each with its own evidence trail and narrative.
13
Checklist

Generate the document checklist

Where Article 9's baseline and Article 10's ratings come together into one list.

You click Generate checklist. If either article is still incomplete, a banner names exactly what's missing and the result is marked DRAFT rather than blocked outright.
You see A grouped, editable table of required documents — each tagged baseline (from Art. 9), risk add-on (triggered by an Art. 10 rating), or both, with the reasoning spelled out per row. You can rename, remove, add a document manually, or leave a remark on any line.
Produces A saved, timestamped generation — the definitive supplier document list for Guatemala this cycle.
14
Checklist

Export

The bottom of the same page — four buttons, no separate export screen.

You click Risk report (docx) · Checklist (docx) · Checklist (xlsx) · Audit extract (xlsx) — any or all of them.
Produces Files in the exports folder, named systematically — KLK_EUDR_Guatemala_2026_risk-report_2026-08-19.docx and so on — with every export logged to the audit trail. A history of past exports for this scope sits just below the buttons.
15
Overview

Back to Overview — done

The natural place to end the story, since it's also where it started.

You see Art. 9 requirements refined: full count. Art. 10 sections signed: 10 / 10. Complete: 100%. The Recent activity card lists every approval and signature from this walkthrough, newest first — the same trail an auditor would see.

What this story makes visible

Guatemala only exists in this walkthrough as a label — the current baseline is Malaysia and Indonesia, and adding a real third country is a scope decision, not just a form. But the mechanics above are exactly what happens for either of them, today, for every cycle.

The route map at the top is the honest shape of the app: two content tabs, one shared inbox. Article 9 and Article 10 each start a research run and end with a decision on their own page — but everything in between passes through Evidence. That's not a detour; it's the one place every piece of evidence, from either article, gets the same human check before it counts for anything.
